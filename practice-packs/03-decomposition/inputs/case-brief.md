# Case brief — Acme Logistics v. Sample Vendor Co.

> **All names, dates, and figures in this brief are fictional. Do not treat it as legal advice.**

## Background

Our client, **Acme Logistics, Inc.** ("Acme"), is a Midwest-based freight broker with annual revenue of approximately $180M. In March 2024 Acme contracted with **Sample Vendor Co.** ("SVC"), a software vendor based in California, to deliver a customer-relationship management platform (the "CRM Platform") tailored to freight brokerage operations.

The contract (the "Agreement") was executed March 4, 2024 between Acme (signed by COO Sarah Chen) and SVC (signed by CEO Marcus Webb). The Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Delaware. The total contracted value was $612,000 over a three-year term, payable in equal quarterly installments of $51,000. Acme paid the first three installments in full ($153,000 total) between March and September 2024.

## What SVC promised

The Statement of Work (SOW), attached as Exhibit A to the Agreement, specified:

1. **Phase 1 — Configuration (Apr 1 – May 15, 2024).** SVC to configure the CRM Platform for Acme's three regional dispatch offices and migrate existing customer records from Acme's legacy system.
2. **Phase 2 — Integration (May 16 – Jun 30, 2024).** SVC to deliver a bidirectional integration between the CRM Platform and Acme's homegrown freight billing system ("FBS").
3. **Phase 3 — Training and go-live (Jul 1 – Jul 31, 2024).** SVC to train 24 named Acme operations staff and certify the system for production use.
4. **Phase 4 — Hypercare (Aug 1 – Aug 31, 2024).** SVC to provide dedicated support during the first month of production use, with a guaranteed response time of two business hours for any defect classified Severity 1 or 2.

The Agreement, Section 9.2, provides that SVC "shall achieve each Milestone on or before the date set forth in the applicable SOW," and Section 11.4 entitles Acme to terminate "for material breach" if any Milestone is missed by more than thirty (30) days and is not cured within fifteen (15) business days of written notice.

## What happened

- **Phase 1** completed two weeks late, on May 29, 2024. SVC did not request a written extension. Acme did not object at the time.
- **Phase 2** was never completed. As of the date of this brief (October 14, 2024), the FBS integration is non-functional. SVC's project manager (Janet Liu) acknowledged in email dated August 18, 2024 that "we under-scoped the FBS work; we'll need an additional 6-8 weeks." That email is the only written acknowledgement; no formal change order was issued.
- **Phase 3** training did not occur on schedule because Phase 2 was incomplete. SVC offered a partial training session on August 5, 2024, which 14 of the 24 named staff attended. No certification was issued.
- **Phase 4 hypercare** has nominally been in effect since August 1, but Acme has logged seventeen (17) Severity 1 or Severity 2 tickets, of which only four (4) received a response within the contractual two-business-hour window. Average response time on the remaining thirteen tickets was 4.2 business days.

## Acme's losses

- **Direct damages.** Acme has paid $153,000 against a system that does not meet the contracted specification.
- **Cover damages.** Acme spent $41,000 on temporary contractor labor to manually bridge the missing FBS integration between July and October 2024.
- **Operational damages.** Acme estimates lost productivity at approximately $80,000-$120,000 attributable to the failed integration (this figure is internal and not yet documented to a litigation standard).

## Acme's position

Acme wants to:

1. Terminate the Agreement.
2. Recover the $153,000 paid plus the $41,000 in cover damages.
3. Be released from any remaining payment obligation ($459,000 over the remaining nine quarters).

Acme would prefer a negotiated settlement over litigation. Acme's CEO would accept a settlement that recovers at least $150,000 cash, releases Acme from the Agreement, and includes a mutual non-disparagement clause. The CEO would walk into litigation only if SVC offers less than $75,000.

## What we are sending to SVC's counsel

A pre-suit demand letter setting out the facts, the legal theory, the demand, and a deadline for response. We need that letter today.
